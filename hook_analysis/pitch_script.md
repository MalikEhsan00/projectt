# Pitch script: Hook-based creator briefs (prototype), about 2.5 minutes

**[Open: the problem, ~20 s]**
Sohan, I want to show you a prototype I've been building. Right now, when we brief creators, we're mostly guessing which opening works. The first three seconds decide whether anyone keeps watching, and we have thousands of videos with real sales data that could tell us what works. We just weren't using it.

**[Step 1: label every hook, ~30 s]**
So I took our creator-video export, almost ten thousand videos, with GMV, views, and engagement. I sorted every video's opening line into eight hook types: Problem, Curiosity Gap, Social Proof, Bold Claim, Direct Question, Story, Offer, and Other. Then I ranked those hooks by GMV, views, and engagement.

**[Step 2: what we found, honestly, ~40 s]**
Here's the important part. At first, Curiosity Gap looked like the clear winner. But when I checked, one single video was eighty-two percent of its sales. So I re-ran it without the top videos. The honest picture: Bold Claims and Offers are the most dependable, with about forty-one percent of those videos making money. Curiosity is a high-risk, high-reward bet. Story and Social Proof openers are the weakest. That check is why I trust this more than a simple leaderboard.

**[Step 3: creator types and briefs, ~35 s]**
Now, we can't write a custom brief for thousands of creators. So I grouped them into three types based on the hooks they already use. The Relatable Parent, the Promo Seller, and the Show-and-Tell creator. Each type gets its own brief template: the hook to use, the beat-by-beat structure, the call to action, and what to avoid. The script fills in each creator's handle, our product, and the current deal. It generated over three and a half thousand briefs in seconds.

**[Be clear it's a prototype, ~30 s]**
To be clear, this is a prototype, not the finished product. Three limits. First, only about nine percent of videos had transcripts, so most labels came from captions, which are a weaker signal. Second, it's one product, and many hooks have small samples. Third, most creators only have one or two videos, so many type assignments are low confidence.

**[The ask, ~20 s]**
What I'd like is your go-ahead to improve it: get transcripts for all videos, test the briefs on a small group of creators, and measure whether they beat our current approach. If they do, we scale it. Thanks.
